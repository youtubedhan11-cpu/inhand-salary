How to Calculate In-Hand Salary from CTC

Your CTC (Cost to Company) is everything your employer spends on you — not what reaches your bank account. To get from CTC to in-hand salary, work through these steps:

  • Step 1 — Remove the employer's hidden parts. If employer PF and gratuity sit inside your CTC, subtract them first. What remains is your gross salary.
  • Step 2 — Find your taxable income. From gross salary, subtract the standard deduction (₹75,000 new regime / ₹50,000 old regime) and, in the old regime only, your HRA exemption, 80C, 80D and professional tax deductions. Professional tax is not deductible under the new regime.
  • Step 3 — Apply the slab rates for FY 2026-27, then the Section 87A rebate, any surcharge, and the 4% health & education cess. That gives your total income tax for the year (your monthly TDS is roughly this divided by 12).
  • Step 4 — Subtract what leaves every month: your employee PF contribution and professional tax.
  • Step 5 — Divide by 12. Gross − tax − employee PF − professional tax, divided by 12, is your monthly in-hand salary.

Example: on a ₹12 lakh CTC (50% basic, PF capped, employer PF + gratuity inside CTC), gross salary is ₹11,49,540. Taxable income in the new regime is ₹10,74,540 after the ₹75,000 standard deduction (professional tax is not deductible under the new regime, though it is still deducted from your pay) — the tax on it (₹47,454) is fully wiped out by the Section 87A rebate, so income tax is zero and in-hand comes to ₹93,795 a month. Change any input above and watch each number move — that is the fastest way to learn your own payslip.

New vs Old Tax Regime FY 2026-27: Which Is Better?

The new regime offers lower slab rates, a ₹75,000 standard deduction, and a Section 87A rebate that wipes out tax on taxable income up to ₹12 lakh — but it gives up almost all deductions. The old regime keeps higher rates but lets you claim HRA exemption, 80C (₹1.5 lakh), 80D and more, with a ₹50,000 standard deduction.

Choose the new regime if you pay little or no rent, have few investments in 80C, or your CTC is up to about ₹12.75 lakh (where new-regime tax is zero). For most salaried employees without a home loan or large HRA, the new regime wins — the verdict line on the calculator above tells you by exactly how much.

Choose the old regime if your deductions are large: high rent with a big HRA component, full ₹1.5 lakh of 80C, health premiums, and home-loan interest can together cut taxable income by ₹4–6 lakh, which can beat the new regime's lower rates — typically at higher incomes. Run both numbers before you declare your regime to payroll; salaried employees can switch every year.

Why Is In-Hand Salary Less Than CTC?

Because CTC is the employer's total cost, and several parts of it never pass through your monthly bank credit:

  • Employer PF — the company's EPF contribution is inside CTC but goes straight to your PF account.
  • Gratuity — about 4.81% of basic is set aside inside CTC and paid only after 5 years of service.
  • Employee PF — your own 12% (or capped ₹1,800/month) is deducted from gross pay. It is still your money, just locked in EPF.
  • Income tax (TDS) — deducted monthly based on your projected annual tax.
  • Professional tax — a small state tax, roughly ₹200/month in states that levy it.
  • Variable pay / bonus — counted in CTC but paid once or twice a year, not monthly.

Add these up and a ₹12 lakh CTC commonly becomes roughly ₹9.8–11.3 lakh of actual annual take-home depending on the regime and PF mode. The gap is not your employer cheating you — it is how the CTC is packaged. Always negotiate and compare offers on in-hand, not CTC.

Frequently asked questions

The questions every salaried Indian Googles in April.

Is a ₹12.75 lakh salary tax-free in FY 2026-27?

Yes, under the new regime. A gross salary of ₹12.75 lakh minus the ₹75,000 standard deduction gives a taxable income of exactly ₹12 lakh. At that level the Section 87A rebate (up to ₹60,000) cancels the entire tax, so your income tax is zero. Just above ₹12 lakh taxable, marginal relief ensures your tax never exceeds the amount by which your income crosses ₹12 lakh.

What is the standard deduction for FY 2026-27?

₹75,000 under the new regime and ₹50,000 under the old regime. It is subtracted automatically from your salary income — no bills or proof needed.

Does CTC include employer PF and gratuity?

In most Indian offer letters, yes. Employer PF (12% of basic or capped at ₹1,800/month) and gratuity (~4.81% of basic) are usually counted inside the CTC figure, which is why this calculator lets you toggle both. Check your offer's CTC breakup — if they are listed inside the total, keep the toggles ON.

What are the new-regime tax slabs for FY 2026-27?

0–₹4 lakh: nil · ₹4–8 lakh: 5% · ₹8–12 lakh: 10% · ₹12–16 lakh: 15% · ₹16–20 lakh: 20% · ₹20–24 lakh: 25% · above ₹24 lakh: 30%. Plus 4% cess, and a surcharge of 10% / 15% / 25% when taxable income crosses ₹50 lakh / ₹1 crore / ₹2 crore. Budget 2026 left these slabs unchanged from FY 2025-26.

Is PF deducted from my in-hand salary?

Yes — the employee's PF share is deducted from gross salary every month. With the wage-ceiling option it is ₹1,800/month; on full basic it is 12% of basic. The employer's matching share is separate and normally sits inside your CTC.

Which regime should I choose?

For most people without large rent/HRA and deductions, the new regime is better — and it is the default if you don't choose. If your HRA exemption + 80C + 80D + home-loan interest together exceed roughly ₹4–5 lakh, run the old regime numbers above; the verdict line shows the exact saving either way.

What is professional tax?

A small tax levied by some states on salaried employees, typically around ₹200 per month (₹2,400 a year, approximate — it varies by state and slab). States like Delhi, UP, Haryana, Punjab and Rajasthan do not levy it. It is deducted from your pay in both regimes, but it is deductible from taxable income only under the old regime — not under the new regime.
